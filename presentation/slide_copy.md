## 1. Under Armour and FitLife Brands

**A disciplined acquisition option to test recurring customer demand**

PwC x Goizueta 2026 case | October 2026

## 2. Recommendation

**Investigate FitLife; the modeled 25%-premium price needs more synergy evidence**

- Illustrative equity offer $97m at an assumed 25% premium to an indicative October 22 capitalization; this is **not a recommended bid**.
- Fund equity 50% cash and 50% nonvoting UA Class C shares. Retire ~$38m target debt.
- Standalone DCF EV is ~$104m versus ~$134m offer EV: require >$30m risk-adjusted net synergy value plus integration costs.
- Approve diligence and a pilot now; negotiate only after the evidence gates clear.

*Source: FTLF June 2026 10-Q; UA June 2026 10-Q. Premium, structure and fees are assumptions.*

**Full-price customer demand remains weak despite an improving operating outlook**

## 5. FitLife's footprint

**FitLife adds a ~$101m LTM nutrition business with repeat-purchase potential**

FY2025 revenue $81.46m plus H1 2026 $51.87m less H1 2025 $32.06m equals $101.27m LTM revenue. The same bridge gives $11.00m LTM operating income. FitLife owns brands including MusclePharm and Irwin Naturals. Preserve existing channels initially.

*Source: FTLF FY2025 10-K and June 2026 10-Q. LTM is derived.*

## 6. The diligence problem

**Acquisitions drove FitLife's headline growth while legacy brands fell**

Legacy FitLife revenue declined 23% year over year in Q2 2026. Irwin contributed $14.14m of quarter revenue after its 2025 acquisition. Require brand-level unit sales, customer retention and retailer concentration before an offer.

*Source: FTLF Q2 2026 earnings release.*

## 7. Customer test

**A controlled store pilot can distinguish new demand from channel switching**

Run a 90-day test in 20 matched UA stores and selected ecommerce placements. Compare treated and control markets on new-to-UA customers and combined gross profit after acquisition costs. Check repeat purchase at 180 days before widening distribution. Use adult products that pass independent quality and claims review.

*Pilot design is a proposal, not an observed result.*

## 12. Stage gates

**Each expansion decision requires evidence of new customers and positive contribution**

1. Diligence: organic sales, certification, net debt, creditor terms and a board cash floor.
2. Pilot: randomized 20-store comparison and ecommerce cohort measurement.
3. Scale: expand only after a positive incremental contribution and repeat purchase hurdle.

UA's previous fitness-app purchases and exits make an explicit stop rule important.

*Source: case section on past deals; stage gates are proposed.*

## 13. Risks

**Supplement quality and claims are conditions of ownership**

| Risk | Gate |
|---|---|
| Athlete contamination or unsupported claims | Independent testing, label audit and substantiation before UA endorsement |
| Existing FitLife customers shift checkout | Track customer identity and combined contribution versus controls |
| Organic brand erosion | Verify SKU and cohort retention; reprice or walk |
| Liquidity and management distraction | Seasonal cash floor, separate P&L and board checkpoints |

*Sources: FDA supplement guidance, FTC health-products guidance, USADA; FTLF Q2 release.*

## 14. Opportunity cost

**The acquisition must clear its valuation gap and outperform roughly $90m of internal spending**

The cash offer, target debt repayment and fees total about $90.6m. Standalone DCF EV is $29.8m below the modeled offer EV before fees and integration. A comparable internal program could fund product work and customer acquisition. Compare both on incremental gross profit and retained new customers after a pilot or partnership test.

*Cash commitment is model-derived. Internal program is a counterfactual assumption.*

## 15. Board ask

**Authorize diligence and pilot; do not approve the 25%-premium offer today**

Proceed to a binding offer only if FitLife's organic brand trend can stabilize, product safety and claims pass independent review, financing preserves the approved cash floor, and risk-adjusted net synergies exceed the ~$29.8m standalone value gap plus fees and integration. Otherwise continue UA's core brand and footwear repair without this acquisition.
