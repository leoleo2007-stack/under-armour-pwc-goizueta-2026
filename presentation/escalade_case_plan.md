# Escalade acquisition case: execution plan

**Selected target:** Escalade, Inc. (NASDAQ: ESCA). **Buyer:** Under Armour.
**Status:** Research and presentation work plan, not a completed valuation or financing commitment.
**Information cutoff:** October 22, 2026. **Submission:** October 28, 2026, 5 p.m. Eastern; confirm against the [case brief](../case/brief.md).

Start with the [Escalade screen](../research/escalade_screen.md). Existing FitLife inputs, model, recommendation and slide copy remain prior-target research; they must not be presented as Escalade results. The earlier $700 million financing discussion concerned another candidate and is not an Escalade purchase-price assumption.

## Thesis to test

Acquiring Escalade could give Under Armour additional relationships with sports participants and opportunities to earn incremental apparel and footwear profit. The case must establish why ownership creates more value than a commercial partnership, and whether that value exceeds the premium and integration costs.

Focus the initial commercial thesis on ONIX, basketball equipment and Lifeline. Assess the entire acquired portfolio, including less adjacent businesses. Do not assume all brands can be renamed Under Armour or that licensed brands transfer without consent. Do not assume divestiture proceeds fund closing.

## How to adapt the reference deck

The supplied Altice slides demonstrate an analytical sequence: business diagnosis, normalized earnings, valuation, transaction mechanics, and before/after financial outcomes, supported by detailed appendices. Use that sequence and evidence discipline. This acquisition case needs shareholder value, acquisition financing, customer economics and integration analysis. Replace restructuring-specific liquidation and creditor-recovery waterfalls with acquisition-value and cash-flow bridges.

Use original writing and graphics; do not reproduce the reference screenshots in the submission.

## Work steps and deliverables

### 1. Lock the scope and constraints
Confirm that sports equipment satisfies the case's different-industry requirement. Record the fact cutoff, assumed closing date, forecast periods, slide limit and anonymous/team PDF requirements. Keep eligibility as an unresolved gate until confirmed.
**Deliverable:** one-page scope and assumptions sheet.

### 2. Write the investment hypothesis
Answer: What problem does Under Armour face? Why Escalade? Why buy rather than partner? At what price and financing mix does the deal create value?
Separate the benefits of owning Escalade's existing earnings from actual incremental synergies.
**Deliverable:** a one-paragraph thesis with explicit disconfirming evidence.

### 3. Build a traceable source register
Collect three annual periods and the latest available quarters for both companies, earnings releases, proxy ownership, credit agreements, acquisition disclosures and material licenses. Include Escalade's August 2026 ASL transaction. Record publication date, period, URL, page, units and any adjustment for each input.
Classify every model input as reported fact, calculation or assumption. Use only information available at the cutoff.
**Deliverable:** source register and historical financial inputs.

### 4. Diagnose Under Armour's customer problem
Establish revenue, category, geography, channel and margin trends from filings. Identify the customer or distribution gap Escalade could address. Distinguish customer overlap from truly incremental reach.
**Deliverable:** buyer problem slide with measurable baseline and acquisition objective.

### 5. Map Escalade's portfolio and competition
Map products, customer groups, distribution, owned versus licensed rights and strategic fit. Review STIGA and adidas-related rights and change-of-control provisions where disclosed. Compare competitors by product category; valuation peers need not be direct product competitors.
Keep unknown brand revenues or profits explicitly unknown rather than inventing segment disclosures.
**Deliverable:** portfolio/competition map and licensing diligence list.

### 6. Normalize historical earnings
Reconcile reported operating income, EBITDA, management adjustments and the team's normalized earnings. Examine tariff recoveries, acquisitions, impairments and other nonrecurring items individually. Do not subtract a recovery again from a metric already excluding it.
**Deliverable:** reported-to-normalized earnings bridge with source references.

### 7. Forecast both businesses independently
Build five-year income statements, balance sheets and cash flows before synergies. Align Under Armour's March fiscal year with Escalade's December year using a documented calendarization method.
An assumed January 2027 close and 2027–2031 forecast is a modeling option, not a known transaction timetable.
Use category forecasts only where disclosure supports them; otherwise use consolidated drivers with transparent assumptions.
**Deliverable:** standalone forecasts with base, upside and downside drivers.

### 8. Build synergies from operating drivers
Revenue synergy = reachable customers × new-to-UA share × conversion × net annual spending.
Translate revenue into incremental contribution after product costs, discounts, returns, marketing, fulfillment and cannibalization.
Cost synergy = eligible cost base × achievable reduction × implementation ramp.
Model one-time integration costs, continuing investment, taxes and working capital separately. Avoid counting the target's existing sales, intercompany sales or standalone improvements as synergies.
**Deliverable:** synergy schedule with evidence, accountable owner, ramp and sensitivities.

### 9. Value Escalade and set a maximum offer
Triangulate standalone DCF, comparable companies and relevant precedent transactions. Explain peer differences in size, growth, margins and business mix.
Unlevered free cash flow = after-tax operating profit + depreciation/amortization − capex − increase in operating working capital.
Bridge enterprise value to equity value consistently for debt, cash and other claims. Show standalone value, net synergy value, proposed consideration and retained value for UA shareholders.
The maximum offer must leave sufficient value and downside protection for UA; do not automatically transfer all synergy value to sellers.
**Deliverable:** valuation range, per-share offer and walk-away price.

### 10. Construct financing and sources and uses
Uses: equity purchase consideration, debt refinancing where required, transaction/financing fees and necessary closing liquidity.
Sources: usable buyer cash, new borrowing and issued shares. Treat target cash consistently and do not count it twice.
Compare cash/debt, stock and mixed consideration. Test minimum operating cash, borrowing availability, covenant restrictions, interest coverage, debt maturities and share dilution. Distinguish equity purchase price, enterprise value and total funding required.
**Deliverable:** balanced sources and uses plus proposed financing terms, all uncommitted assumptions labeled.

### 11. Model the combined company
Combine the standalone forecasts and add synergies, financing, purchase accounting, amortization, integration costs and share issuance. Reconcile opening and closing balance sheets, cash and debt.
Compare no-deal UA, acquisition without synergies, base acquisition and downside acquisition. Report incremental cash flow, acquisition returns, leverage, liquidity and EPS effects; EPS accretion alone does not establish value creation.
Stress weaker sales, lower margins, delayed synergies, higher interest expense and slower inventory conversion.
**Deliverable:** five-year combined model and downside liquidity analysis.

### 12. Plan integration and test ownership
First 100 days: preserve customer service, retain critical staff, validate licenses and establish reporting.
Months 4–12: run measurable customer-conversion pilots and implement substantiated procurement or overhead savings.
Years 2–3: scale successful pilots and assess portfolio performance.
Assign an executive owner and KPI to each initiative. Compare acquisition against licensing, partnership and internal investment on cost, speed, control and expected value.
**Deliverable:** integration timeline and ownership-versus-partnership decision.

### 13. Build the 15-slide main story
1. Title and recommendation.
2. Under Armour's strategic problem.
3. Why Escalade and why ownership.
4. Target portfolio and strategic fit.
5. Customers, market and competition.
6. Incremental customer acquisition economics.
7. Historical performance and earnings normalization.
8. Standalone operating forecast.
9. Synergies and integration costs.
10. Valuation, offer and maximum price.
11. Sources and uses and financing.
12. Combined financial outcomes and shareholder value.
13. Downside liquidity, key risks and mitigants.
14. Integration plan and partnership comparison.
15. Final recommendation and conditions to proceed.

Keep the title within the main-slide limit; verify all submission rules against the brief.

### 14. Build the appendices
Include historical statements, trailing-period calculations, normalization reconciliations, forecast assumptions, peer selection, precedents, DCF sensitivities, synergy calculations, detailed sources and uses, debt/covenant review, purchase accounting, combined statements, downside cases, ownership/licenses and source register.
Main slides should communicate conclusions; appendices should let a judge reproduce the work.

### 15. Design for legibility and consistency
Use a consistent grid, restrained colors, descriptive chart titles, visible units and source footnotes. Distinguish actuals, forecasts and assumptions. Use original charts and link every displayed financial result to the model.
Do not copy the reference deck's dense appendix layout onto every main slide.

### 16. Audit and rehearse
Verify sources equal uses, balance sheets balance, cash/debt roll forwards reconcile, enterprise/equity values bridge and slide numbers match the model. Check cutoff compliance and remove team identifiers from the anonymous PDF and its metadata.
Prepare answers to: Why buy rather than partner? Why acquire unrelated categories? How many customers are truly new? Why this premium? What happens at half the assumed conversion? Can UA afford the downside? What causes us to walk away?
**Deliverable:** audited model, final deck, two PDFs and rehearsed Q&A.

## Decision gates

Proceed only if industry eligibility is resolved, material brand/license rights are viable, the proposed price leaves value for UA, downside liquidity is adequate, and ownership benefits exceed a plausible partnership alternative. Until those tests are complete, Escalade is the selected research target rather than an unconditional acquisition recommendation.
