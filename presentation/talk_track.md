# Fifteen-minute presenter notes

This is a **working argument** for the team to interrogate, not a finished submission. The main deck has 15 slides including the title. Place the separate meet-the-team slide only in the identified PDF. Use source footnotes on every evidence slide, and label all deal and adoption figures as assumptions.

| Slide | Time | What to say and show |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 0:30 | State the decision: investigate FitLife Brands as a conditional acquisition by Under Armour. Name the customer-demand problem. |
| 2 | 1:00 | State the illustrative $134m enterprise value, roughly $97m equity offer, and the three gates: organic demand, product safety, and price. This is a request for diligence, not approval of a signed deal. |
| 3 | 1:00 | Show FY2026 sales $4.97b, North America down 7.9%, footwear down 11%. Explain that June-quarter operating income of $47m and adjusted FY2027 guidance do not establish sales recovery: June-quarter revenue fell 3%. Use case Exhibits 1–3 and the UA filings. |
| 4 | 0:45 | Show the screen. A target must have customer frequency, a different industry, and a purchase price UA can finance. Xponential's debt and Laird's preferred claim make common equity value misleading; GoPro already has a definitive transaction. |
| 5 | 0:55 | Introduce FitLife's nutrition brands and LTM $101.3m revenue. The strategic asset is repeat consumption and distribution, not a claim that every UA buyer wants supplements. Cite FTLF filings. |
| 6 | 1:05 | Confront the hardest fact: legacy FitLife brands declined 23% in Q2. Acquired Irwin drove consolidated growth. Ask whether stabilized cohorts and SKU economics justify ownership before placing a bid. |
| 7 | 1:00 | Describe a 20-store treatment/control pilot and selected ecommerce placement. Sell independently reviewed adult products with clear labeling. Measure new-to-UA buyers and incremental combined gross profit, not transferred FitLife orders. |
| 8 | 1:00 | Walk through the Year 3 assumption bridge: $17.28m store sales plus $7.70m online sales before overlap; 70% net-new yields $17.49m. Point out the online order denominator uses all DTC revenue and is generous because DTC includes stores. |
| 9 | 0:55 | Show downside/base/upside incremental operating contribution of ($2.39m)/$3.47m/$9.33m. State that a negative pilot result stops expansion. None are management forecasts. |
| 10 | 1:05 | Show sources and uses: $48.51m cash and $48.51m assumed nonvoting Class C stock to sellers, $38.1m target debt retired, $4m assumed fees. June cash $396m plus target cash $1.1m leaves $306.5m; UA debt stays $591.2m. Check covenants and seasonal cash before any offer. |
| 11 | 0:50 | State the historical arithmetic: FY2026 UA plus FitLife LTM revenue $5.07b; combined operating loss $152m before synergies and accounting adjustments. This is not GAAP pro forma because the periods differ. |
| 12 | 0:55 | Explain three decision stages: diligence, randomized pilot, then rollout after a contribution and retention hurdle. UA's fitness-app purchase and exit history requires a stop rule and accountable owner. |
| 13 | 0:55 | Make supplement quality a deal gate. FDA does not approve supplements for safety/effectiveness before sale; claims need substantiation and tested athletes face contamination risk. Review labels, adverse events, testing, and marketing practices before UA endorsement. |
| 14 | 1:00 | Compare the roughly $90m cash commitment with investing that cash in footwear and North American marketing. The internal option is the real competitor; no public data yet proves the acquisition beats it. |
| 15 | 0:45 | Ask the board to approve diligence and a controlled pilot framework, conditional on transaction access. No binding offer until organic sales, safety, cash floor, and incremental customer economics clear the gates. |

The times total about **14 minutes**; leave the remaining minute for handoffs. Each speaker should own a claim and the source behind it. A judge may ask why a pilot cannot precede an acquisition. Answer honestly: a commercial partnership would reduce capital risk, but may not offer control or economics; the team should test whether ownership adds enough value to justify its cost. If a pre-acquisition pilot is feasible, run it first.
