# Escalade challenger screen — October 22, 2026 information cut-off

**Status:** A stronger sports-product link than FitLife, but the case's “different industry” rule is an interpretive risk. Escalade manufactures and distributes sporting equipment, while Under Armour sells apparel and footwear. Ask the organizers whether sporting equipment qualifies before choosing it as the final target. This is a research screen, not a recommendation to pay the modeled price.

## Why consider it

- Escalade owns or distributes ONIX pickleball equipment, Goalrilla basketball goals, Lifeline fitness products, STIGA table tennis equipment, Bear Archery and Brunswick Billiards, among others. ONIX/Goalrilla/Lifeline can support a **play + train + wear** proposition with UA footwear/apparel. The broad archery, billiards and lawn-game portfolio also creates distraction. Some brand rights, notably STIGA North America, are licensed; the 10-K lists adidas Fitness, requiring contract review for a UA combination. [Escalade 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/33488/000143774926006094/esca20251231_10k.htm)
- It already sells through sporting goods and mass retailers as well as online. U.S. sales were $228.611m of $240.158m in 2025, so its geography matches UA's North American problem. This **does not establish customer transfer**: wholesaler sell-through data and opt-in customer identities may be unavailable. [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/33488/000143774926006094/esca20251231_10k.htm)
- FitLife offers a more frequent consumable purchase and a lower purchase price; Escalade offers a more direct sports participation and equipment story. Neither acquisition alone repairs UA footwear demand.

## What must be proven

1. **Eligibility:** Sporting equipment may count as a different industry from apparel, but “sporting goods” could be read as too close to sportswear. Obtain an organizer ruling before building a final deck around it.
2. **New customers:** Track whether ONIX paddle or Goalrilla hoop buyers purchase *incremental* UA products. Escalade's equipment purchases may be infrequent, and retailer-controlled channels limit customer-level cross-selling.
3. **Brand rights:** Identify owned versus licensed products, adidas Fitness and STIGA terms, change-of-control consent and any competitor exclusivity.
4. **Financial quality:** Strip the tariff refund out of operating profit; separate Gold Tip acquisition from organic category growth; test 2025 sales decline, concentration (two customers accounted for 19% and 11% of 2025 revenue), tariffs and import exposure.
5. **Capital alternative:** Compare a ~$151m cash commitment (cash offer, debt repayment and fees) plus ~40m UA shares with a partnership or internal footwear investment. Do not assume that whole-company ownership is needed to run a co-marketing pilot.

**Preliminary verdict:** Escalade is a **stronger finalist than FitLife for strategic coherence**, subject to a defensible interpretation of the industry rule and a much tougher price/capital hurdle. It should replace FitLife only if the team can show measurable UA customer acquisition from equipment buyers and why the unrelated portfolio is worth owning.
